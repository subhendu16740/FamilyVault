FamilyVault go-to-market

How FamilyVault finds its first thousand families, written the way you would write a design doc. The problem is an incident report, the customer is a type, the funnel is a pipeline with named events, and the rollout ships behind gates.

The strategy on one screen

read this if you read nothing else

In an emergency a family cannot reach its own paperwork. It lives in one person's head and in three or more inboxes, apps and a cupboard.

Salaried, 25 to 38, in a metro, parents in another city, holding several policies and investments. NRIs with parents in India come next.
promise
Anyone in the family can ask “What is Papa's health policy number?” and get the answer and the document in seconds, by voice and in Hindi too.

Family emergency readiness. Competing as “document storage” means losing to free Google Drive and DigiLocker.

The problem, as an incident report

Lead every conversation with the problem, not the product. Written as a postmortem, the problem is easy to state precisely, and the precision is what makes the marketing credible.

INC-0214
A family cannot retrieve a health policy during a hospital admission
SeverityHigh: care and money at risk
Detected byMaa, at the hospital desk
Single point of failureRohan, two states away
  1. 02:14Papa is admitted. The TPA desk asks for the health card or policy number, and a photo ID.
  2. 02:14Maa messages Rohan in Bengaluru: “Insurance ka policy number maang rahe hain. Kahan rakha hai??”
  3. 02:16Rohan searches his personal inbox for “policy number”: 2,847 results.
  4. 02:19The group cover documents were in his previous employer's mailbox. Access ended when he left.
  5. 02:23The policy schedule PDF is password protected.
  6. 02:26The insurer's app sends its OTP to Papa's phone. Papa is on a stretcher.
  7. 02:31The paper original is in the almirah in Lucknow.

Impact, measured

The emergency is the story. The unclaimed money is the proof that the problem is systemic and not anecdotal.

“In many cases, families are simply unaware that such assets exist.”

Two more numbers for the narrative. In a June 2025 survey of more than 5,000 people by CoverSure, an insurance vendor, 60% of dependents did not know they were covered by any policy, and only one in ten could describe their cover. And the clock at the hospital is real: the TPA must be told within 24 hours of an emergency admission, and IRDAI expects a cashless decision within an hour of a complete request. Neither helps a family that cannot produce the health card and an ID.

Root cause

Each cause below is a property of a badly designed system, which is why the fix is a product and not a reminder to be more organised.

In engineering terms

Treat it like a B2B product sold to an admin. The young professional is the admin and buyer: they set up the vault and pay. The parents are the end users. The family is the beneficiary. 04

Positioning: the interface contract

Positioning decides who you are compared with. Choose the comparison you can win.

As “document storage”, FamilyVault is compared with Google Drive and DigiLocker, which are free, and the comparison is about gigabytes. As family emergency readiness, the comparison becomes “can my family get this at 2 AM without me?”, and nothing else on the list answers yes.

Positioning statement

For salaried Indians who carry their family's paperwork in their head and their inbox, FamilyVault is the family document vault that lets anyone you trust ask for a policy, ID or statement in plain words, typed or spoken, in English or Hindi, and get the answer with the document attached. Unlike a Drive folder, DigiLocker or a chat with yourself, it reads your documents, knows who “Papa” is, and tells you before anything lapses.

The alternatives, judged on the job

AlternativeWhat it is good atWhere it fails at 2 AM
Doing nothingFree, zero effortWorks until the day it doesn't. This is the real competitor.
Google Drive or Dropbox folderFree storage; Drive reads text in PDFs and images for search; Gemini can answer questions on paid plansSomeone has to build and share the folder. Parents won't navigate it. No reminders.
DigiLockerOfficial copies of government-issued documentsOne account per person. Not a shared, searchable family vault.
WhatsApp “note to self”, family groupFamiliar and instantBuried in chat history, tied to one phone, no structure
Spreadsheet of policy numbersA good indexNo documents, goes stale, rarely on the parents' phone
Password manager (e.g. 1Password Families, ~$72/yr)Credentials and emergency kitsBuilt for logins, not reading documents
Legacy and nominee apps (Legaci, Asset Pass, One Inheritance, UnClaimedX)Asset registers for heirsDesigned for after a death; manual entry; no OCR, questions or Hindi voice on their public pages

Why we win, ranked by how defensible it is

1. Family-native

A family tree with the Hindi words for each relation (Dadi or Nani, Tau or Chacha, Bua, Bhabhi), people with or without an account, and search that understands relations: “Nani's pension”, “Mummy ka PAN”. Moderately defensible, because a vault full of a family's documents and people is sticky.

2. Built for Indian parents

Spoken questions and answers read aloud, offered in Hindi and eight more Indian languages on the web (the browser's voices decide which work), Indian ID formats, Indian scripts in OCR. Moderately defensible.

3. Readiness, not storage

An emergency card for each person, and reminders 90, 30 and 7 days before an expiry on every phone that asks, today; a readiness score and a family drill next (section 10). Defensible once built, because nobody else is framed this way.

4. Ask instead of search

Answers with the source attached. Weak on its own: Gemini and ChatGPT can already answer questions over files. Never lead with “AI”.

What FamilyVault is not: a will or estate planner, a password manager, or a portfolio tracker. Saying so keeps the promise small enough to keep.

House style

  • Be specific. Policy numbers, 2 AM, Papa. Avoid “peace of mind” and “seamless”.
  • Show relief more than fear. The film spends as long on “Tum so jao” (go back to sleep) as on the crisis.
  • Lead with the outcome. “Ask for Papa's policy” beats “AI-powered retrieval”.
  • Claim only what the code does (section 9). One false security claim costs more than every true one earns.
  • Write for two readers. The admin reads the ad; a parent may be the one reading the WhatsApp forward.
06

The funnel is a pipeline

Each stage has a definition, an event that proves it happened, and a pass rate. Overall throughput is the product of the pass rates, so the cheapest growth is usually a fix to the worst stage, not more traffic at the top.

The north star: Ready families

ready_family(vault) :=
      documents(vault)  >= 8
  AND categories(vault) >= 3
  AND EXISTS member m <> creator(vault):
        joined(m) AND retrieved(m)   -- a sourced answer to their question, or a document opened

Counting uploads measures effort. Counting questions measures curiosity. Neither says whether the family is ready. The last clause is what matters: someone other than the uploader has found something on their own.

In engineering terms

A backup you have never restored is not a backup. A vault nobody else has opened is not ready. Build the restore test into onboarding as a family drill: once the essentials are in, prompt the admin to have Maa ask for one document herself. It proves access works, and it is the single best predictor of retention you can instrument on day one.

Tracking plan

The app has no analytics today: nothing in src/ sends product events. Add them before spending anything, because you cannot improve a stage you cannot see. PostHog fits well (open source, web and React Native SDKs, can be self-hosted); an append-only events table written by an Edge Function also works.

Privacy rule for events: send counts, categories and timings only. Never document text, file names, names, numbers or the question itself. Unanswered questions are a key quality signal: log source_count = 0, not the question.

07

Growth loops

A funnel is a line; a loop feeds itself. FamilyVault has a natural loop because the product only works when the family is in it.

The viral arithmetic

k = invites per vault × acceptance rate × share of joiners who start their own vault. With 3 × 60% × 15%, k ≈ 0.27. That is below 1, so it will not grow on its own, but every 100 families you acquire become about 100 / (1 − 0.27) ≈ 137. Put differently, it cuts your effective acquisition cost by about 27%. Measure the three factors separately; each has its own fix.

Compliance you can market

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified on 13 November 2025, and the core obligations apply from 13 May 2027. The ones that matter here: a plain-language notice and consent, reasonable security safeguards (penalties up to ₹250 crore), breach notice to users and the Board with a detailed report within 72 hours, contracts with every processor, and verifiable parental consent for anyone under 18 (family vaults will hold children's birth certificates). Section 14 of the Act lets a person nominate someone to exercise their rights on death or incapacity, which is almost exactly the emergency access feature this product should have anyway. UIDAI tells entities not to keep Aadhaar copies unless the first eight digits are masked, and the ingest pipeline currently extracts full Aadhaar numbers into metadata. Mask them by default and say so publicly.

Pricing

Decided and built since v1 (038–044). What remains is testing it, and one rule about trust.

Free

  • 1 GB for the family, 4 members who sign in
  • Scan, add and ask, the family tree, emergency cards
  • Reminders on every phone, share links
  • 10 voice chats for each person

★ Family Plus: ₹100 a month or ₹1,100 a year

  • 10 GB, still 4 members
  • Voice chats with no limit
  • The Reminders page, every expiry date in one list
  • Import from Gmail
  • $10 or $110 abroad; paid by UPI, card or net banking through Razorpay, and nothing renews by itself

The rule: a lapse never costs a family a document without warning. Today, 30 days after Family Plus ends, the newest documents above the free 1 GB are removed (040), after a notice when it ends and warnings 7 days and 1 day before; until then the family can read, search and download everything. That is defensible for storage nobody pays for, but it is the opposite of v1's “never hold documents hostage”, so make it impossible to miss: a one-tap download of everything from each warning, and these exact terms on the pricing page.

Test the price with what the Plus page already knows: which ★ feature brought each family there (/plus?feature=), and who then pays monthly or yearly. Ask price questions in interviews too (too cheap, a bargain, getting expensive, too expensive). For reference, Trustworthy charges $10–40 a month in the US, Everplans $99.99 a year, and 1Password Families about $72 a year.

Unit economics to watch, with the cost terms measured per family per month once analytics exists: LTV = ARPU × gross margin × expected lifetime in months. The variable costs are storage and egress, Groq tokens per question, Hugging Face embedding calls, OCR.space pages, and Razorpay's 2% plus GST on each payment. Aim for LTV / CAC ≥ 3 and payback in under 12 months.

Interview script, short version

  1. Tell me about the last time someone in your family needed a document urgently. What happened?
  2. Where do your policies and statements live today? How many places?
  3. If you were in hospital tonight, what would your parents or spouse do first?
  4. What have you already tried? Why did you stop?
  5. Who else in the family would need to be in this for it to work?

Ask about the past, not about hypothetical futures. “Would you use this?” produces polite noise; “what did you do last time?” produces data.

  • Ready families (the north star), total and new
  • Invites per vault and acceptance rate
  • Unanswered-question rate (source_count = 0)
  • 30-day family retention by weekly cohort
  • Cost per Ready family, by channel, once paid starts

The experiment template

  • Hypothesis: changing X will move metric Y, because Z
  • Metric: one primary metric, fixed in advance
  • Size: the smallest change worth acting on
  • Sample: per variant, from the formula below
  • Decision rule: what you will do if it wins, loses or ties
-- visitors per variant for 80% power at a 5% false-positive rate
n ≈ 16 · p · (1 − p) / δ²

-- baseline sign-up rate 10%, detect a 3-point lift
n ≈ 16 · 0.10 · 0.90 / 0.03² = 1,600 per variant

Below that traffic, don't A/B test. Talk to users instead: at small scale, five interviews teach more than a statistically meaningless test.

Answer quality is a marketing metric. A first question that comes back empty ends the trial. Review the unanswered rate every week, and fix retrieval before buying traffic.
